# Viral Video Remix

Create a short-form edit that keeps the source clip central while adding a clear, original brand contribution. Treat trend selection, editing, rights, and publishing as separate decisions.

## 1. Identify the inputs

- Confirm the target platform, audience, goal, and requested deliverables from the conversation.
- Find the source clip in the user’s attachments, files, or supplied URL. If the user asks for trend research, check current platform trend sources and record the date checked.
- Discover the brand from its website and provided logo/assets. Follow the existing brand video or brand guidelines skill when available. Do not assume a palette, font, voice, or logo from another brand.
- Use user-provided assets when available. If the user asks for a specific edit and the source or essential brand assets are missing, ask only for those missing inputs.
- For each candidate, record its original URL, creator handle, post date, visible engagement counts, and any available views. Distinguish observed facts from estimates.

## 2. Find a useful trend candidate

When asked to find trends, look for platform-native trend pages and authorized analytics or discovery tools available in the current environment. Search current web sources if needed. Prefer trends that fit the brand’s audience and can be adapted with a meaningful point of view.

Assess:
- **Fit:** Can the trend connect naturally to the brand or audience?
- **Freshness:** Is it still active, based on recent posts and current platform signals?
- **Momentum:** Are views or interactions rising relative to the creator’s usual baseline when that information is available?
- **Adaptability:** Can the brand add a useful joke, insight, reaction, or story without distorting the source?
- **Rights and safety:** Is there clear permission, a platform-native remix option, or another valid reuse basis?

Do not describe a clip as viral or trending without evidence. Do not present scraped or estimated metrics as official. Public profile access and commercial reuse rights vary by platform and tool; use only connected, authorized sources and follow their terms. If evidence is limited, label the candidate a hypothesis and say what is unknown.

## 3. Choose a remix style

Use the source footage as the main visual. Add a distinct brand contribution that is understandable with sound off. Pick a style that suits the clip; create three variations when the user asks to compare directions.

- **Editorial frame:** Keep the clip prominent with refined top and bottom brand panels, a concise hook, and restrained logo placement.
- **Reaction or commentary:** Add a short brand voice, reaction beat, or on-screen comment timed to the source. Keep the source legible and avoid covering the speaker.
- **Statement card:** Use a bold, short headline or punchline around the footage, with kinetic type and a clean branded end card.
- **Native duet or stitch:** If appropriate and supported, prefer the platform’s own remix feature so the source context and attribution remain intact.

Make variations meaningfully different in layout, hook, pacing, and brand expression. Do not make three near-identical color swaps. Keep claims truthful and make the brand’s role obvious without pretending the brand created the source clip.

## 4. Edit for mobile viewing

- Default to a vertical 9:16 canvas at 1080 × 1920, unless the platform or source requires another format.
- Keep the hook in the opening moment, and make the story clear in a quick sequence: hook, source moment, brand reaction or value, next step.
- Keep key faces, subtitles, logos, and calls to action out of platform interface areas. Preserve the creator’s handle and any source watermark; do not crop or cover attribution.
- Use brand type and color for the added elements only. Keep text short, high contrast, and readable on a phone. Avoid clutter and decorative effects that compete with the source.
- Add accurate captions for spoken words. Use platform-safe audio or music and retain dialogue clarity.
- Use a restrained logo placement and a clear branded close. Avoid claiming endorsement by the original creator.
- Prefer a native editing or video workflow already available. For programmatic edits, use a timeline-based editor or motion graphics with FFmpeg for assembly and encoding. Preserve the original audio unless the user requests a change.
- Export a high-quality MP4 suitable for the selected platform. Name variations clearly and provide a preview/contact sheet when several options are delivered.

## 5. Rights, attribution, and publishing

A credit line does not by itself grant permission to reuse someone else’s video. Before preparing an edit for public reposting, establish one of these: the user owns the footage, has permission or a license, or is using an applicable platform-native remix feature in line with its terms. If none is clear, keep the work as a private style mockup or use licensed/owned footage, and state the limitation.

When publishing is requested:
- Confirm the destination account and verify that the connected publishing tool supports the requested format and feature.
- Use the source creator’s correct handle and the user’s approved caption. Do not invent permission or creator endorsement.
- Check current platform requirements for trial distribution or API publishing instead of assuming that a feature is exposed.
- Ask before publishing if the user only requested drafts, previews, or style exploration. Do not schedule or publish as an implied next step.
- Save the final caption, hashtags, source link, creator credit, and rights basis with the export.

## 6. Review before delivery

Watch the complete render, including its opening and end. Check that:
- The story and added brand contribution are clear and the source footage remains recognizable.
- Captions match the audio; text, logo, and faces are readable and not hidden by app controls.
- Branding is accurate, the source creator remains identifiable, and the edit does not imply endorsement.
- Audio is audible and not clipped; cuts, overlays, and transitions are clean.
- The MP4 plays, has the intended aspect ratio and duration, and has no accidental black frames or missing media.
- Any trend claims, engagement figures, attribution, and CTA are supported and correct.

Deliver the MP4(s), a brief description of each variation, the source credit/caption when requested, and any material rights or trend-data limitation.
